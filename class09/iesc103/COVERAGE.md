## 2. Concept-by-Concept Mapping

### Concept 3.1: Plant vs Animal Tissues & The Meristematic Growth Engine
- **Textbook Sections:** 3.1, 3.2, 3.2.1, 3.2.2, 3.2.3
- **Coverage Details:**
  - Division of labour and cellular specialisation in multicellular organisms.
  - Divergent evolutionary architectures: stationary autotrophs (rigid cell walls, dead supportive tissues, indeterminate localized growth) vs motile heterotrophs (flexible cell membranes, living tissues, determinate uniform growth).
  - Meristematic cytology: thin primary cellulose walls, dense cytoplasm, prominent nuclei, lack of large sap vacuoles.
  - Apical meristem (SAM & RAM) driving vertical primary elongation.
  - Lateral meristem (vascular & cork cambium) driving secondary radial girth expansion and annual growth rings (Dendrochronology Table 3.7).
  - Intercalary meristem driving nodal and internodal regrowth in grasses and sugarcane.
- **NCERT Activity:** Activity 3.1 (Onion root tip excision experiment).
- **Interactive Simulation:** `sim-meristems` (Interactive growth lab with apical, lateral cambial rings, intercalary regrowth, and root tip excision toggle).

### Concept 3.2: Simple Permanent Tissues: Parenchyma, Collenchyma & Sclerenchyma
- **Textbook Section:** 3.2.4
- **Coverage Details:**
  - Cellular differentiation from meristems into permanent tissues.
  - Parenchyma: isodiametric living cells, thin cellulose walls, large intercellular spaces, turgor maintenance, food storage.
  - Chlorenchyma (chloroplast-containing photosynthetic mesophyll) and Aerenchyma (hydrophyte buoyancy air chambers).
  - Collenchyma: living elongated cells with irregular pectin corner thickenings, mechanical flexibility without breaking under wind shear.
  - Sclerenchyma: dead cells at maturity, heavily lignified secondary walls, narrow lumina, fibers (coconut husk coir) and sclereids (nutshells/stone cells).
- **NCERT Activity:** Activity 3.2-A (Microscopic transverse section of stem).
- **Interactive Simulation:** `sim-simple-tissues` (Dynamic wind flex test, sclerenchyma brittle snap test for Exercise 9, turgor inflation).

### Concept 3.3: Plant Protective Armor & The Dual Vascular Engine
- **Textbook Section:** 3.2.4
- **Coverage Details:**
  - Epidermis: single-layered protective pavement, waxy cutin cuticle, stomatal apparatus with bean/kidney-shaped guard cells, root hairs.
  - Cork (Phellem): secondary protective tissue formed by cork cambium; dead, suberized waterproof and gas-impermeable armor.
  - Complex Permanent Tissues: heterogeneous cell types working as a unified functional unit.
  - Xylem: Tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma (sole living component), xylem fibers. Unidirectional transpiration pull.
  - Phloem: Sieve tubes (enucleate living cells), companion cells (nucleated metabolic directors), phloem parenchyma, phloem fibers (sole dead bast component). Bidirectional source-to-sink translocation.
- **NCERT Activity:** Activity 3.2-B (Rhoeo leaf peel mount and stomata observation).
- **Interactive Simulation:** `sim-vascular-transport` (Dual vascular engine: transpiration suction vs sugar mass-flow translocation, elephant debarking phloem girdling simulation).

### Concept 3.4: Animal Epithelial Tissues: Cellular Shields, Secretory Linings & Cilia
- **Textbook Section:** 3.3.1
- **Coverage Details:**
  - Universal epithelial architecture: tight cell junctions, continuous sheets, avascularity, basement membrane.
  - Simple Squamous: ultra-thin pavement cells (0.3 μm) for instantaneous diffusion in lung alveoli and blood capillaries (Fick's Law).
  - Stratified Squamous: multi-layered strata with keratinized dead surface scales resisting abrasive wear and mechanical tear in skin and oesophagus.
  - Simple Cuboidal: cube cells with microvilli brush borders for secretion and tubular reabsorption in nephrons and glands.
  - Columnar & Ciliated Columnar: tall pillar cells with basal oval nuclei, goblet cells, and rhythmic cilia beating to drive the respiratory mucociliary escalator.
- **NCERT Activity:** Activity 3.2-C (Human cheek squamous epithelium smear).
- **Interactive Simulation:** `sim-epithelial-barriers` (Membrane diffusion chamber: alveolar gas exchange vs skin abrasion armor vs ciliated escalator).

### Concept 3.5: Connective Tissue Matrix: Fluid, Skeletal, Dense & Loose Frameworks
- **Textbook Section:** 3.3.2
- **Coverage Details:**
  - Connective tissue cytology: cells widely separated within an abundant extracellular matrix (fibers and ground substance).
  - Fluid Connective Tissue: Blood plasma (55%), Erythrocytes (RBCs), Leukocytes (WBCs), Platelets (thrombocytes), and Lymph.
  - Skeletal Connective Tissue: Hard mineralized Bone (calcium phosphate matrix, Haversian systems, osteocytes) vs resilient Cartilage (chondrin matrix, chondrocytes in lacunae).
  - Dense Regular Connective Tissue: Tendon (muscle to bone, white collagen fibers, high tensile strength, limited elasticity) vs Ligament (bone to bone, yellow elastic fibers, joint stabilization).
  - Loose Connective Tissue: Areolar tissue (subcutaneous repair and organ support) and Adipose tissue (adipocytes storing fat globules, thermal insulation).
- **NCERT Activity:** Activities 3.2-D & 3.3 (Blood smear differential count & connective tissue microphotographs).
- **Interactive Simulation:** `sim-connective-matrix` (Tensile load test: tendon pull vs ligament sprain, bone vs cartilage shock, blood smear counter).

### Concept 3.6: Muscle Motors & Neural Communication: Striated, Smooth, Cardiac & Neurons
- **Textbook Sections:** 3.3.3, 3.3.4
- **Coverage Details:**
  - Contractile proteins: actin and myosin sliding filament mechanism.
  - Striated / Skeletal Muscle: voluntary, cylindrical, unbranched, syncytial multinucleated, cross-striations, rapid contraction, fast fatigue.
  - Smooth / Visceral Muscle: involuntary, spindle-shaped (fusiform), uninucleated, non-striated, sustained peristaltic waves in viscera.
  - Cardiac Muscle: involuntary, cylindrical, branched, uninucleated, intercalated discs with gap junctions, 40% mitochondrial volume, tireless beating without fatigue.
  - Nervous Tissue: Multipolar motor neuron anatomy (cyton/soma, dendrites, axon, myelin sheath, Nodes of Ranvier, synaptic knobs, neurotransmitters).
- **NCERT Activity:** Activity 3.5-A (Comparative cytology of muscle tissues and spinal motor neuron).
- **Interactive Simulation:** `sim-muscle-neuron` (Cardiac tirelessly beating syncytium, skeletal twitch, smooth peristalsis, neuron saltatory action potential propagation).

### Concept 3.7: The Musculoskeletal Engine & Articular Joints: Biomechanics & Motion
- **Textbook Sections:** 3.4, 3.4.1, 3.5
- **Coverage Details:**
  - Integrated musculoskeletal machinery: bones as rigid levers, joints as fulcrums, muscles as contractile engines.
  - Body mass distribution: ~40% skeletal muscle, ~15% bone mass (Activity 3.4).
  - Antagonistic muscle pairs: biceps flexor vs triceps extensor (muscles can only pull, never push).
  - Articular joint architecture:
    - Ball and Socket Joint: 360° multi-axial freedom in 3 planes (shoulder and hip).
    - Hinge Joint: uni-axial angular motion in a single plane (knee, elbow).
    - Pivot Joint: axial rotational turning around a peg (neck atlanto-axial joint).
    - Fixed / Immovable Joints: cranial sutures interlocking skull bones.
  - Deceleration physics: Normal bent-knee jump ($\Delta t = 0.20$ s) vs Straight-leg stiff jump ($\Delta t = 0.015$ s) impact force ($F = \Delta p / \Delta t$).
- **NCERT Activities:** Activities 3.4 & 3.5-B (Body mass proportions & arm antagonistic muscle action).
- **Interactive Simulation:** `sim-joints-biomechanics` (Normal vs straight-leg jump impact physics, ball-and-socket 360° rotation, pivot joint rotation).

13. **Exercise 13 (Inquiry):** Vibha vs Neha: meristems located only at apices? Socratic questions uncovering lateral cambium and intercalary nodes.

15. **Exercise 15 (Critical Inquiry):** Refuting "Each plant tissue performs only one specific function" through multifunctional parenchyma, epidermis, and xylem.
