KEEP THE BIG PICTURE

Your chapter, on one page.

DNA copying

Copy + apparatus → 2 cells

Copying errors → variation → evolution's raw material.
Asexual modes

1 parent → clones

Fission · fragmentation · regeneration · budding · spores · vegetative.
Sexual reproduction

½ + ½ → full set

Meiosis halves DNA; fusion restores it; combinations are novel.
Flowering plants

Pollen → stigma → seed

Ovule → seed · ovary → fruit. Pollination ≠ fertilisation.
Humans

Oviduct → uterus → placenta

Fertilisation in oviduct; implantation in uterus; ~9 months.

One-line test: asexual reproduction copies one genome; sexual reproduction mixes two half-genomes; in humans the placenta exchanges food and waste through blood, without mixing maternal and foetal blood.

Three-minute exit ticket
  1. Amoeba, Planaria piece, Hydra bud, Rhizopus spore, potato eye, mustard seed: sort each into its mode of reproduction.
  2. A papaya orchard has only female trees and no males nearby. Will it set fruit by itself? Explain using pollination and fertilisation.
  3. Why does the uterus lining break down when the egg is not fertilised?
Check your explanation

1. Amoeba — fission; Planaria piece — regeneration; Hydra bud — budding; Rhizopus — spore formation; potato eye — vegetative propagation; mustard seed — sexual reproduction. 2. No: papaya is unisexual, so female flowers need pollen from a male plant; without pollination there is no fertilisation, no zygote, no seed or fruit. 3. The thick, blood-rich lining is prepared to nourish an embryo; with no fertilised egg to receive, it is shed as blood and mucous (menstruation).
