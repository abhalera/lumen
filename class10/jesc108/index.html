KEEP THE BIG PICTURE

Your chapter, on one page.

Variation grows

Asexual: small · Sexual: large

Each generation inherits old differences and adds new ones.
Dominance

TT, Tt → tall · tt → short

One T suffices; t shows only in double copy.
F2 ratios (large numbers)

3 : 1 phenotype · 1 : 2 : 1 genotype

Expectations over hundreds of offspring — never a promise for one family.
Independence

9 : 3 : 3 : 1

Seed shape and colour sort independently; new combos appear.
Gene → trait

DNA → protein → character

Two gene sets (one per parent); one set per gamete; chromosomes carry them.
Sex (humans)

Mother: X · Father: X or Y

XX girl, XY boy — about 50 : 50 across large numbers.

Ratios are large-number expectations, not family guarantees: Mendel counted hundreds of pea plants to see 3:1, and human 50:50 for boys and girls only emerges across many births.

Three-minute exit ticket
  1. A tall F1 plant (Tt) is self-pollinated and gives exactly 4 seeds. Must one be short? Explain.
  2. A farmer crosses two seed traits at once. Name one offspring combination neither parent showed, and explain where it came from.
  3. A couple has three daughters. What is the chance the next child is a boy, and who contributes the deciding chromosome?
Check your explanation

1. No — 3:1 describes hundreds of offspring; each of the 4 seeds is independently 75% tall / 25% short, so 4 tall (or any mix) is possible. 2. E.g. tall with wrinkled seeds (or short with round seeds): the two gene pairs sorted independently into gametes and recombined. 3. Still about 50%; each birth is independent, and the father’s sperm carries the deciding X or Y.
