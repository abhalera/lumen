Your chapter, on one page.

Distance

PQ = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²)

OP = √(x²+y²). Swapped order agrees.
Shapes

√50 + √2 vs √52 ⟹ right

Sides √34 ×4, diagonals √68 ×2 ⟹ square.
Collinear

3√2 + 2√2 = 5√2

Longest leg = sum of two shorter.
Section

((m₁x₂+m₂x₁)/(m₁+m₂), (m₁y₂+m₂y₁)/(m₁+m₂))

Midpoint = average. Equidistant locus: x − y = 2.

Distance is Pythagoras with named legs; take the positive root. Collinearity is AB + BC = AC, not an area formula — this rationalised chapter has no triangle-area formula. m₁ weights the far end B. External division waits for higher classes.

Three-minute exit ticket
  1. Derive PQ from RS = x₂−x₁ and QT = y₂−y₁ in three lines.
  2. Why does 3√2 + 2√2 = 5√2 prove Ashima’s row is straight?
  3. (4,−3)–(8,5) at 3:1 — where is P, and why does m₁ weight B?
Check your explanation

1. PQ² = (x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)² by Pythagoras in ΔPTQ. 2. Middle distances sum exactly to the longest leg. 3. (7, 3); cross-multiplying m₁/m₂ = (x−x₁)/(x₂−x) sends m₁ to x₂’s side.
