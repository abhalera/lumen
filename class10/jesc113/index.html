KEEP THE BIG PICTURE

Your chapter, on one page.

Ecosystem

Biotic + abiotic, interacting

Producers → consumers → decomposers; gardens to ponds.
Trophic levels

1 producers · 2 herbivores · 3 small carnivores · 4 large

Each step is “who eats whom”, arrows point to the eater.
10% energy model

10,000 → 1,000 → 100 → 10 J

Lindeman ~10% average approximation (model, real 5–20%); rest not transferred to next level (heat, respiration, waste, uneaten) — chains stay short.
Biomagnification

Non-degradable → concentrates upward

Top-level humans carry the maximum pesticide load.
Ozone

O₂ + UV → O₃ shield

CFCs thinned it; 1987 UNEP freeze began the repair.
Waste rule

Biodegradable → compost · rest → segregate

Enzymes are specific: biology cannot digest plastic; e-waste goes to authorised collection.

Energy flows one way and shrinks ~10× per level (model); poisons do the opposite and grow upward; ozone shields above while waste must be sorted below.

Three-minute exit ticket
  1. Grass → goat → human: name each trophic level, and predict what happens to humans if goats vanish.
  2. Starting from 10,000 J in producers, how much reaches a third-level carnivore in the 10% model — and where did the rest go?
  3. Your dustbin holds peels, a plastic bottle and a dead torch battery. Sort all three and give the battery’s exact destination.
Check your explanation

1. Grass T1 producer, goat T2 primary consumer, human T3 secondary consumer here. If goats vanish, that chain collapses for humans — other chains/foods must fill the gap; no level can be removed without effects rippling. 2. T1 10,000 → T2 1,000 → T3 100 J; ~90% per step is not transferred to the next level (released as heat, used in respiration and movement, lost as waste or left uneaten). 3. Peels → compost pit/bin; bottle → dry recyclables; battery → e-waste/hazardous collection centre (never burnt, buried casually, or opened at home).
