KEEP THE BIG PICTURE

Your chapter, on one page.

Names

linear · quadratic · cubic by degree 1 · 2 · 3

ax+b · ax²+bx+c · ax³+bx²+cx+d, leading ≠ 0.
Zeroes

p(k) = 0 ⟺ graph meets the x-axis at k

Linear: one (−b/a). Quadratic: ≤ 2. Cubic: ≤ 3.
Quadratic Vieta

α + β = −b/a · αβ = c/a

2x²−8x+6: 1+3 = 4, 1×3 = 3.
Cubic Vieta

sum −b/a · pairs c/a · product −d/a

2x³−5x²−14x+8: 5/2 · −7 · −4.

Zeroes are x-intercepts: lines cross once, parabolas cut twice, touch once or miss, cubics wiggle at most three times. Coefficients already know the zeroes — Vieta reads them back. Plotting graphs is not required of students and is not evaluated (NCERT footnote).

Three-minute exit ticket
  1. Why does 1/(x − 1) get no degree name?
  2. x² − 3x − 4 has which zeroes? Which table row proves it?
  3. For cubic ax³ + bx² + cx + d, state all three Vieta relations with signs.
Check your explanation

1. The variable is in a denominator — not a polynomial. 2. −1 and 4; Table 2.1 rows y = 0. 3. sum −b/a, pairs +c/a, product −d/a (minus alternates).
